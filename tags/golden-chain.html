GC-02.01 · George Odom’s Equilateral Midline & Circumcircle Chord (1983)

Let ABC be an equilateral triangle inscribed in circle cO, and let L and M be the midpoints of sides AB and AC. Extending midline LM to meet circumcircle cO at X and Y divides the chord in the golden ratio: \(|LM|/|MY| = |LY|/|LM| = \phi\).

GC-07.02 · John Molokach’s Golden Rhombus from Shifted 1×3 Rectangles

Stack two \(3 \times 1\) rectangles RECT1 and RECT2 shifted by one unit around center O, draw unit circle cO, and draw the two tangents to cO perpendicular to outer diagonal R1R6. Together with the horizontal outer edges, they bound a golden rhombus ABCD (\(|AC|/|BD| = \phi\)) with \(|T1K|/|KA| = \phi\).

GC-05.03 · Prasolov & Bùi Quang Tuân’s Equi-Area Irregular Pentagon

Let ABCDE be any convex pentagon—regular or irregular—whose five peripheral triangles \(\triangle ABE, \triangle ABC, \triangle BCD, \triangle CDE, \triangle DEA\) have equal area. Then every diagonal is parallel to the opposite side and is divided by the crossing diagonals in the golden ratio, e.g. \(|BM|/|MD| = \phi\).

Requiring symmetric upper and lower circles cU1, cU2 to be simultaneously tangent to all five circles uniquely forces the flanking circles to have exact radius \(\phi\), outer radius \(\phi^3\), and \(|OC4|/|C4A4| = \phi\).

GC-07.06 · Exact φ-Limit Resolution of the Hooper–Curry Fibonacci Dissection

Replace integer Fibonacci lengths \((3, 5, 8, 13)\) in the \(8 \times 8 = 64\) vs. \(5 \times 13 = 65\) dissection fallacy by exact powers \((1, \phi, \phi^2, \phi^3)\). In rectangle ABCD of width \(\phi^3\) and height \(\phi\), internal vertices Q \(= (\phi, 1/\phi)\) and P \(= (\phi^2, 1)\) lie identically on diagonal AC, forming the 4-point golden chain \(|AQ|/|QP| = |AP|/|AQ| = \phi\).
