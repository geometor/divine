GC-01.01 · Euclid’s Classical Golden Section (Elements II.11 & VI.30)¶

Discoverer(s): Euclid of Alexandria | Date: c. 300 BCE | Field: \(\mathbb{Q}(\phi)\) | Loci: 3 (Lemoine \(S = 9\))

Given a straight segment AB, erect the square ABCD, bisect side AD at M, and transfer MB along ray DA beyond A to E. Swinging AE onto AB at G cuts AB in extreme and mean ratio, so that \(|AB|/|AG| = |AG|/|GB| = \phi\).

Given & Constructive Steps¶

Given: Endpoints A at \((0, 0)\) and B at \((2, 0)\) defining a base segment AB of length \(2\).

step Erect square ABCD on base AB with D at \((0, 2)\) and C at \((2, 2)\).
step Bisect vertical side AD at midpoint M \(= (0, 1)\) and join diagonal-radius MB of length \(\sqrt{5}\).
step Draw compass circle cMB centered at M through B, cutting line AD extended below A at E \(= (0, 1 - \sqrt{5})\).
step Draw circle cAE centered at A through E, cutting AB internally at G \(= (\sqrt{5} - 1, 0)\) and completing the golden square AGFE.

Exact Symbolic Proof & Certificate¶

In right triangle \(\triangle MAB\), \(Q(M, A) = 1\) and \(Q(A, B) = 4\), so quadrance \(Q(M, B) = 5\) and \(|ME| = |MB| = \sqrt{5}\). Subtracting \(|MA| = 1\) gives \(|AG| = |AE| = \sqrt{5} - 1 = 2/\phi\), while the remainder on AB has length \(|GB| = 2 - (\sqrt{5} - 1) = 3 - \sqrt{5} = 2/\phi^2\). Hence \(Q(A, G) = 6 - 2\sqrt{5}\) and \(Q(G, B) = 14 - 6\sqrt{5}\), satisfying \(Q(A, G) = (\phi + 1)\,Q(G, B)\) and \(|AG|/|GB| = \phi\).

Invariant

\(Q(A, G) = 6 - 2 \sqrt{5}\), \(Q(G, B) = 14 - 6 \sqrt{5}\), \(Q_{\text{long}}/Q_{\text{short}} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2} + \frac{3}{2} = \phi^2\)

\(\mathbb{Q}(\phi)\)

Historical Commentary & Attribution¶

In Book II Proposition 11 of the Elements, Euclid formulates this construction purely in the language of geometric algebra—requiring that rectangle \(AB \cdot GB\) equal square AGFE—and returns to it in Book VI Proposition 30 using theory of proportions (Euclid). In Alexander Bogomolny’s survey (Cut-the-Knot), this construction anchors all subsequent minimal-step refinements.
