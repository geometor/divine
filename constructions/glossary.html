Glossary¶

Classical Euclidean, Projective & Rational Trigonometry Invariants

Definitions of classical Euclidean, projective, and Rational Trigonometry concepts used throughout the catalog.

Golden Ratio (φ) (Sectio Aurea / Divina Proportione)

The positive root φ = (1 + √5)/2 ≈ 1.6180339887 of x² − x − 1 = 0, satisfying φ² = φ + 1 and 1/φ = φ − 1. Appears in: GC-03.01, GC-03.02, GC-03.03, GC-03.04, GC-03.05, GC-03.06, GC-03.07, GC-03.08, GC-04.01, GC-04.02, GC-04.03, GC-04.04 (+19 more)

Golden Section (Collinear Division in φ)

A collinear point triple (A, G, B) whose consecutive segments stand in the golden ratio |AG|/|GB| = φ (so |AB|/|AG| = φ as well). Appears in: GC-01.01, GC-01.02, GC-01.03, GC-01.04, GC-01.06, GC-01.07, GC-02.01, GC-02.02, GC-02.03, GC-02.04, GC-02.05, GC-02.06 (+32 more)

Extreme and Mean Ratio (ἄκρος καὶ μέσος λόγος (Euclid VI.Def.3))

Euclid’s classical definition: a straight line is cut in extreme and mean ratio when, as the whole line is to the greater segment, so is the greater to the less. Appears in: GC-01.01, GC-05.01

Golden Chain (Connected Collinear φ-Progression)

A sequence of four or more collinear points in which overlapping triples form consecutive golden sections (such as lengths 1/φ, 1, 1/φ or 1, φ, φ²). Appears in: GC-01.02, GC-01.03, GC-01.05, GC-02.01, GC-02.07, GC-03.01, GC-03.07, GC-03.08, GC-05.01, GC-05.03, GC-05.05, GC-06.03 (+5 more)

Quadrance (Q(A, B) = (x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)

Norman Wildberger’s Rational Trigonometry invariant measuring squared separation without square roots; two segments stand in ratio φ iff Q_long / Q_short = φ + 1 = φ². Appears in: GC-01.01, GC-01.02, GC-01.07, GC-02.02, GC-02.03, GC-02.04, GC-02.06, GC-03.03, GC-03.04, GC-03.08, GC-04.02, GC-04.03 (+2 more)

Géométrographie (Lemoine Construction Complexity)

Émile Lemoine’s 1902 operational metric counting elementary straightedge (R₁, R₂) and compass (C₁, C₂, C₃) operations; drawing a line through two points or a circle centered at one point through another each contributes simplicity 3. Appears in: GC-01.03, GC-01.04, GC-01.07

Vesica Piscis (Twin-Circle Lens (Euclid I.1))

The symmetric lens formed by two congruent circles each passing through the other’s center, generating √3 altitudes and bisecting their common radius. Appears in: GC-01.03, GC-01.04, GC-01.05, GC-01.06, GC-06.03

Mascheroni Construction (Compass-Only Construction)

A geometric construction using circles alone (0 straightedge lines), proved universal for all Euclidean constructible points by Georg Mohr (1672) and Lorenzo Mascheroni (1797). Appears in: GC-01.05

Rusty Compass (Fixed-Aperture Compass)

A compass locked to a single invariant radius throughout a construction, studied by Abu’l-Wafa, Leonardo da Vinci, Poncelet, and Steiner. Appears in: GC-01.06

Power of a Point (Euclid III.35–37 / Radical Invariant)

For a point P and circle O(r), the invariant quantity |PO|² − r², equal to the signed product |PA|·|PB| along any secant PAB (and to |PT|² along a tangent PT). Appears in: GC-02.05, GC-04.01, GC-06.02, GC-06.06

Intersecting Chords Theorem (Euclid Elements III.35)

If two chords AB and CD of a circle intersect internally at M, then |AM|·|MB| = |CM|·|MD|. Appears in: GC-02.01, GC-03.01, GC-06.04

Butterfly Configuration (Crossed Cyclic Chords across a Midpoint Chord)

Cyclic quadrilateral diagonals crossing a reference chord symmetrically, inducing projective cross-ratio and golden-section divisions. Appears in: GC-02.07

Kepler Triangle (1 : √φ : φ Right Triangle)

The unique right triangle whose side lengths (and side quadrances 1 : φ : φ²) form a geometric progression. Appears in: GC-04.02, GC-06.05

Mixtilinear Incircle (Two-Side & Circumcircle Tangent Circle)

A circle internally tangent to two sides of a triangle and internally tangent to its circumcircle. Appears in: GC-07.05

Golden Rhombus (Diagonal Ratio |d₁|/|d₂| = φ)

A rhombus whose long and short diagonals stand in the golden ratio φ; thirty congruent golden rhombi bound Kepler’s rhombic triacontahedron. Appears in: GC-07.02

Golden Rectangle (Side Ratio φ : 1)

A rectangle whose length-to-width ratio equals φ, characterized self-similarly by leaving a congruent-proportioned golden rectangle when a square gnomon is removed. Appears in: GC-03.07, GC-06.05, GC-07.06
