GC-04.02 · Johannes Kepler’s Geometric-Progression Right Triangle (1597)¶

Discoverer(s): Johannes Kepler | Date: 1597 | Field: \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\phi})\) | Loci: 2 (Lemoine \(S = 6\))

Divide hypotenuse AB at H in the golden ratio (\(|AH|/|HB| = \phi\)), and erect perpendicular HC to semicircle cOB on diameter AB. Right triangle ABC has side lengths in geometric progression \(1 : \sqrt{\phi} : \phi\) and side quadrances in progression \(1 : \phi : \phi^2\).

Given & Constructive Steps¶

Given: Golden-sectioned hypotenuse A \(= (0,0)\), H \(= (\phi, 0)\), B \(= (\phi^2, 0)\) with midpoint O \(= (\tfrac{\phi^2}{2}, 0)\).

step Draw circle cOB on diameter AB of length \(\phi^2 = \phi + 1\).
step Erect altitude HC perpendicular to AB at golden division point H to meet cOB at C \(= (\phi, \sqrt{\phi})\).
step Join AC and BC to complete right triangle ABC.

Exact Symbolic Proof & Certificate¶

By Euclid VI.8 and VI.13, altitude HC is the geometric mean \(|HC|^2 = |AH|\cdot|HB| = \phi \cdot 1 = \phi\). Thus \(Q(B, C) = 1 + \phi = \phi^2\), \(Q(A, C) = \phi^2 + \phi = \phi^3\), and \(Q(A, B) = \phi^4\). Because \(\phi^2 + \phi^3 = \phi^2(1 + \phi) = \phi^4\), Kepler’s two ‘treasures of geometry’—the Pythagorean theorem and the golden section—unite uniquely in \(\triangle ABC\).
