GC-05.01 · Claudius Ptolemy’s Regular Pentagon & Pentagram (Almagest I.10)¶

Discoverer(s): Claudius Ptolemy, Euclid of Alexandria | Date: c. 150 CE | Field: \(\mathbb{Q}(\phi, \sqrt{10 - 2\sqrt{5}})\) | Loci: 6 (Lemoine \(S = 18\))

In unit circle cO with perpendicular radii OB and OC, let M bisect OB. Circle cMC cuts AB at E with \(|OB|/|EO| = \phi\) and chord \(|CE|\) equal to the side of inscribed regular pentagon CP1P2P3P4, whose pentagram diagonals P1P4, CP2, CP3 cut one another in golden chains \(|P1K2|/|K2P4| = \phi\).

Given & Constructive Steps¶

Given: Unit circle cO centered at O \(= (0,0)\) through B \(= (1,0)\), diameter AB, and perpendicular apex C \(= (0,1)\).

step Bisect radius OB at M \(= (\tfrac{1}{2}, 0)\) and swing circle cMC to meet AB at E \(= (-1/\phi, 0)\).
step Step off chord length \(|CE| = \sqrt{3 - \phi}\) around cO from C to inscribe regular pentagon CP1P2P3P4.
step Draw pentagram diagonals P1P4, CP2, and CP3, intersecting along P1P4 at K1 and K2.

Exact Symbolic Proof & Certificate¶

Right triangle \(\triangle MOC\) has \(|MC| = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}\), so \(|OE| = \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2} = 1/\phi\) (the regular decagon side, Euclid XIII.9–10) and \(|CE|^2 = 1 + 1/\phi^2 = 3 - \phi = 4\sin^2 36^\circ\) (the regular pentagon side). By Euclid XIII.8, every pair of crossing pentagram diagonals cuts each other in extreme and mean ratio, forming the 4-point golden chain \((P1, K1, K2, P4)\).

Historical Commentary & Attribution¶

Ptolemy introduced this streamlined circle-and-midpoint construction in Almagest I.10 to compute his chord table at \(36^\circ\) and \(72^\circ\), dramatically simplifying Euclid’s Book IV Proposition 11.
